Aldo Orvieto’s research grows directly from performance. Interpretation becomes a way to examine sources, recover neglected repertoire, and connect score, gesture, recording, and acoustic space.

## Twentieth-century Italian repertoire

His work on Camillo Togni, Gian Francesco Malipiero, and Niccolò Castiglioni includes manuscript study, source research, modern premieres, and complete recording projects. Within this work, Orvieto gave the 2009 world premiere of the rediscovered 1946 *Piano Concerto* by Bruno Maderna and the first modern performance of Togni’s *Variations* for piano and orchestra.

## Sound and electronics

With Ex Novo Ensemble, SaMPL, and collaborators including Alvise Vidolin, Orvieto has worked with live electronics, spatialisation, prepared or amplified piano, and sound-sensing instruments. Technology is treated as part of the musical form and the act of performance.

## Writing and teaching

His performing activity is complemented by essays, lectures, seminars, and masterclasses. Recurring subjects include twentieth-century Italian piano music, the relationship between analysis and interpretation, German Lied, and the transmission of contemporary repertoire to younger performers.
